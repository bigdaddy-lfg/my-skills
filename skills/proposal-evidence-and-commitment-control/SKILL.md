# Proposal Evidence and Commitment Control

## Release checklist

1. Separate user-confirmed direction, proposed specification, vendor-stated claim, open decision and superseded wording before drafting.
2. Check the user's latest exclusions and delivery model against the complete output. Earlier assistant suggestions cannot override them.
3. Attribute competitor capabilities to current primary pages. A missing feature on a page is not evidence that the business never offers it.
4. Translate competitor marketing into testable requirements, not copied guarantees. Distinguish compliant infrastructure from product certification and source-grounded output from infallibility.
5. Separate automatic analysis from authority to execute. State approval boundaries for payments, budgets, employment, record changes and external commitments.
6. Remove claims of uniqueness, savings, integration availability, legal status or launch readiness without evidence. Label targets and pricing as proposed until approved.
7. Preserve unresolved dependencies, and state what this revision supersedes. Creating or emailing a proposal does not establish approval or project-list entry.

Completion condition: each material capability or promise has a status and evidence basis; no open decision is written as approved.

## Counter-examples

- “Nume markets no hallucinations” does not justify “our AI cannot make mistakes.”
- “Built on SOC-2-compliant infrastructure” does not establish product-level certification.
- “YourBooksToday maintains the books” does not mean incomplete invoices or reconciliation exceptions are impossible.
- “Tax reserve calculated” does not mean money has been transferred.
- A five-service bundle specified by the user must not become a books-only package merely because an earlier assistant proposed one.
